Katherine Johnson

The Woman Who Calculated the Way to the Stars

Johnson's Timeline: A Life of Impact

  • 1918: Born as Creola Katherine Coleman in White Sulphur Springs, West Virginia. Showing an early gift for numbers, she graduated from high school at just 14.
  • 1937: Graduated summa cum laude from West Virginia State College at age 18 with degrees in mathematics and French.
  • 1939: Selected as one of the first three African American students to integrate West Virginia University's graduate school. She paused her studies shortly after to start a family with her first husband, James Goble.
  • 1953: Joined the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA), which later became NASA. She began working as a "human computer" in the segregated West Area Computing section in Hampton, Virginia.
  • 1958 - 1961: Transitioned to the Flight Research Division. She calculated the trajectory for Alan Shepard's 1961 flight, America's first human spaceflight.
  • 1962: Ahead of John Glenn's historic orbital flight, NASA relied on electronic computers. Glenn famously distrusted the new machines and asked engineers to "get the girl" (Katherine Johnson) to manually double-check the computer's math before he agreed to fly.
  • 1969 & 1970: Calculated flight paths for the Apollo 11 moon landing and later worked on emergency calculations for the safe return of the Apollo 13 mission.
  • 1986: Retired from NASA after 33 years of breaking race and gender barriers, having co-authored 26 scientific research papers.
  • November 2015: Awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom, the highest civilian honor in the United States, by President Barack Obama.
  • 2016: Her incredible life story was brought to global prominence through the bestselling book and Oscar-nominated film Hidden Figures, where she was portrayed by actress Taraji P. Henson. NASA also dedicated the Katherine G. Johnson Computational Research Facility in her honor.
  • February 24, 2020: Passed away at the age of 101, leaving behind an enduring legacy as an American pioneer in STEM.
